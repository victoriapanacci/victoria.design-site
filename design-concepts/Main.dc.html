Structure study · 20 concepts

Twenty ways to structure Victoria Panacci’s site.

Same copy, same palette, same restraint as the current prototype. The only variable is structure — what the page is organised around, and what the reader meets first. Every image is a marked placeholder sized for your asset list.

Document-led

The page behaves like a paper the reader already knows how to skim.

01 Dossier Fixed identity column · content reads as one document — Reads senior and calm. Narrower content measure; the left column is dead weight on mobile.
02 Index The whole site is a table of contents — Scannable in three seconds and very confident. Carries almost no persuasion — it assumes the reader already wants you.
05 Ledger Every section is a table — facts in columns, no prose blocks — Unusually honest and very fast to compare. Cold; needs the portrait and one photograph to stay human.
09 The Brief Laid out like the document a producer would write themselves — Speaks the reader's native format and answers procurement early. Looks like paperwork — least likely of the set to be shared for its craft.
11 Spec Sheet Monospace, dark, key–value pairs — reads like documentation — Instantly signals the designer who also ships code. Narrow appeal: a brand-led producer may read it as cold or junior-engineer.
20 Reduction One screen of text. Nothing that is not load-bearing. — The most self-assured thing you could ship, and impossible to build wrong. Gives images nowhere to live — it only works if the writing carries it.

Type-led

Typography does the work; the reader is meant to feel your craft before reading a word.

06 Marquee Project names at poster scale carry the page — Memorable and unmistakably a designer's site. Pushes rate and availability far down; weakest on the producer's practical questions.
15 Editorial Magazine spread — columns, a pull quote, the portrait as a feature — The warmest and most personal option; good if you want to be hired as a person. Slowest to read, and columns are fragile on mobile.
16 Poster One full-height typographic statement, then everything else on one screen — Enormous confidence and a screenshot people remember. Spends a whole viewport before saying anything useful.

Work-led

The projects lead. These are the ones that live or die on the six photographs.

10 Gallery First Work fills the screen; words are captions and a thin persistent bar — The strongest showcase of you as a designer — and the one that most depends on the six photographs being excellent.
08 Timeline One vertical spine, 2017 to now — work and depth hang off it — Proves nine years without a paragraph claiming it. Chronology is your story, not the producer's question; availability has to be pinned separately.
19 Mosaic One grid — facts, work, portrait and rate are all tiles of equal citizenship — Everything is visible at once and it reorders cleanly on mobile. No argument order; the reader assembles the case themselves.

Conversion-led

Built around the producer’s decision, not your story.

03 Calling Card Everything that closes a deal, above the fold, no scroll — Ruthlessly efficient for a producer with 40 seconds. Gives a design-led reader nothing to admire.
12 Their Questions Every heading is a question the producer is already asking — The most persuasive structure here and the easiest to write. Repetitive at length; needs strict discipline on question count.
13 Three Doors The fold is a choice between three engagements, not a scroll — Qualifies the lead and frames budget immediately. Asks the reader to self-select before they trust you — risky if they came in cold.
18 Book First The booking panel is the hero; every section below is evidence for it — Highest conversion intent of the twenty. Presumptuous for a first-time visitor — works best when people arrive already referred.

App-led

The structure itself is the proof that you build, not just draw.

04 Split Screen Pitch holds still on the left; work scrolls on the right — The offer is never off screen. Halves the width available to the work, which hurts wide product shots.
07 One Screen, Tabbed No scroll at all — four tabs swap the panel — Feels like a product, not a brochure, and proves you build. Hides content from anyone who does not click, and search engines see less.
14 Console Persistent left nav, content pane — the site behaves like an app — Demonstrates product thinking in the chrome itself. Heavier to build, and an unusual shape for a site someone forwards by link.
17 Stack Full-width panels that stack on scroll, each one a single idea — Paces the argument and never crowds a screen. Long by construction, and the effect is lost if someone skims.
Bracketed placeholders

Booking URL, response time and the six photographs are marked, not invented. Everything else is your real copy.
