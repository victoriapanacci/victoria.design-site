Twenty directions, reconsidered

Round one mistook quirky for novelty — stickers, price tags, playing cards, tilted type. That reads cheap, and cheap is fatal when the whole pitch is a $1,000 day rate. This round moves the character into typography, proportion, and one dry line of copy, and lets restraint carry the premium.

Same fixed content in every tile, so the only variable is the direction. Still sketches — nothing here is a working page.

What changed

Desaturated palettes with a single restrained accent. Hairlines instead of borders and shadows. Type-led hierarchy — scale and space, not weight and colour. Icons at text scale as marginal marks, never coloured badges.

Where the wit went

Into the copy and one deliberate detail per direction — an italic aside, a figure set too large, a rule that stops short. Mordecai stays in the footer. No props.

Fixed content

Reading the cards

Each lists its palette, typefaces, and a temperament mark — one rule reserved, five expressive. Nothing here reaches five now; that was the problem.
